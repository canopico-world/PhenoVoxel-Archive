Five cetaceans: deeper exterior and behavior studies

Fin whale, gray whale, beluga, narwhal and common dolphin are this manually requested batch. Bodies, local surface detail and regional pigment were revised against actual whole photographs and scoped scientific references. The narwhal uses a distinct credited artist body; the other four are original reconstructions. They remain selected, partly stylized and unmeasured. This is not completed photorealism or independent biological validation.

The same geometry, UVs and materials stay through each finite study. Local posterior strokes, a stroke–glide transition, gray-whale side posture, narwhal supine posture, beluga head/melon changes and a dolphin bank are implemented as partial studies. Complete swimming trajectories, prey handling, suction/filtration, contact, force/flow, acoustics and physiology remain unfinished. No quiet pose is relabeled as feeding.

337 species, zero additions. The other 332 record objects, all identities/taxonomy/photographs/occurrence holds, approved blue/humpback surfaces and the previous five artist corrections are preserved. Schedules remain off. The user reviews these five before any next species.

What was actually compared

Whole licensed organism photographs, the complete beluga melon Figure 1A-C and gray-whale Figure 4A-J were individually viewed. Selected fin-whale methods/results and the complete printed Figures 1-2 and 9 were read/rendered. Narwhal FAO plates, primary supine photographs and scoped tagging/tusk literature were retained from the preceding private assessment. Common-dolphin group GPS tracks give group-movement context; they do not measure this model’s bank angle or individual fluke motion. Water caustics, blue lighting, perspective, white age-related patches and prepared/artist surfaces are not calibrated pigment or morphology.

80 final compiled states individually inspected. Actual version, review link and current-bundle gates are checked before accepting a capture. Software compatibility is the reviewed mode; there is no final WebGL optical review. Rejected drafts and software checks

Earlier numerical skins remained too plain, the dolphin flank pattern sat too low, the gray mottles stretched lengthwise, the dorsal fins were too triangular and the first melon draft only rose upward. Those drafts were revised before final review. The initial artist narwhal had two erupted tusks and problematic eye exteriors: the selected left tusk, closed-tip/eye repairs and original source body/images are documented separately. Its dark blue head and strong flank contrast were reduced through a disclosed runtime material derivative; natural white extent still varies by age and individual.

Two module syntax errors were caught and fixed before accepted rendering. The first historical test run passed 5 of 6 checks because the old v651 contract had no reversible v653 replay; that replay now retains the original hash/view/inventory assertions. The first new focused run completed eight checks before a memory failure during a large typed-array assertion. Bounded comparisons then exposed a neutral-glide round-trip difference; exact stored neutral/tusk restoration repaired it. Initial failures are retained. Passing checks establish software/provenance properties, not biological accuracy.

The first full local run passed 368 of 369 checks. New study timing labels omitted the required word uncalibrated; the annotation was repaired and the unchanged provenance check passed. The corrected complete run is retained separately. Sixteen later gray-whale draft states were rejected because their high-contrast flecks resembled uniform camouflage. Broader, sparser, lower-contrast fields replaced them, and all sixteen replacement states were individually inspected. Anatomy-detail sheet captions were also corrected to say selected focus, since clicking a focus chooses its own camera rather than retaining the preceding axis.

The corrected complete local suite passed 369 of 369 checks. The later unchanged-pose cache and gray-whale pigment adjustment each passed all 11 affected checks; all 17 final cetacean/provenance checks passed after review metadata was set. The 80 actual final-bundle PNGs exactly match their individually inspected accepted compiled candidates. Eight handled GPU initialization errors were captured during the final compiled period; software rendering completed, and no other error was captured in that scope. Frozen published CI and live-site byte/action/image checks are recorded separately. Software checks do not validate living biomechanics.

Fin whale Balaenoptera physalus

Open this study

Changed. Long shallow body, V-shaped flatter rostrum, aft falcate dorsal fin and smaller swept pectorals; distinct right-jaw/chevron fields, local lip relief and short-wavelength skin variation.

Evidence scope. Actual author-hosted PDF, selected methods/results and complete printed p.1233 Figures 1-2 and p.1240 Figure 9 inspected. Seven tagged fin whales, 28 dives, Southern California in 2003. Tag-derived body/fluking signals, not full-skin trajectories. Selected 0.29 Hz comes from reported ascent context; amplitude/envelope/glide timing are authored. No copied paper figure.

Remaining. Fin remains unusually smooth and the head-to-shoulder highlight, eye placement, appendage unions, ventral grooves, blowholes, fluke outline and wet optics are selected and unmeasured.

Open this study

Changed. Selected arched head, low dorsal hump/knuckles, broad flippers, three short throat creases, closed low epibiont exteriors and irregular multiscale gray/pale mottling. Rejected dense camouflage-like flecks; selected broader sparser lower-contrast scar fields.

Evidence scope. Actual publisher methods, Table 1 and whole Figure 4A-J inspected. 53 Oregon sightings in 2016, 17 side-swim events in 6 sightings. Remaining. The surface is still a selected envelope: scar shapes and epibiont distribution, head curvature, flipper roots, notch, natural wear and optical response are unmeasured. No sediment/prey/suction mechanism.

Open this study

Changed. Blunter short lips, distinct rounded melon, fuller white adult torso, cervical relief, low dorsal ridge and broader curved flippers; rostral/posterior melon contour study.

Evidence scope. Actual official Europe PMC XML/publisher selected methods and complete Figure 1A-C inspected. Open this study

Evidence scope. Official full text selected methods/Table 1 read. Common-dolphin: 71 group follows on21 survey days, 2014-2017 San Matias Gulf. Boat GPS at 2 min intervals tracks groups, not individual joint/bank/fluke kinematics. The local bank angle remains authored, not fitted from this source; graph figures not viewed. Remaining. The broad common-dolphin concept and eastern-Pacific long-beaked reference are retained. Beak/eye proportions, hourglass boundaries, root unions, flukes, wet optics and bank angle are unmeasured, without subspecies determination.
