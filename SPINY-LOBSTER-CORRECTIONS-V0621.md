## palinurus-elephas

Continuous spined carapace, separate visible eye ends, curved dentate supraorbital horns, narrow unarmed antennular plate, shorter antennules, six overlapping grooved abdominal plates with selected paired pale spots, five-part tail fan and ten articulated non-chelate walking limbs replace the oval/rod draft.

Red-brown original numerical cuticle with a subdued pale flank, irregularly spaced seated spines, longitudinal leg streaks, paired spots on somites2–5 and a single posterior spot6. Tapered strong antennae and fine fan-ray cues remain on the same model through all actions.

A short authored forward bout with fixed-length limbs and ground-fixed support contacts; local antenna/antennule search gestures, quiet supported rest and mild serial abdominal/fan flex. No full feeding, escape trajectory, forces, calibrated stride or sensory/physiological simulation.

Evidence: The authentic JoJan CC BY3.0 Monaco aquarium photograph was viewed alongside selected Naturalis/FAO species/genus diagnoses. Actual Holthuis1991 PDFpage128 printed119 Fig224 and its anterior-carapace detail were rendered and viewed; selected key text page124 printed115 distinguishes species-level groove/spot characters and male-specific P1 armature. The selected non-chelate final-leg form is not an independent determination of source-photo sex.

Remaining: Carapace/head proportions, fine spine coordinates and density, segment grooves, pleural contours, antennal joints, eye microanatomy, maxillipeds and optical properties remain simplified and unmeasured. Ten supported limbs and a mild local tail flex are educational articulation studies, not observed/fitted species kinematics. Female-specific final-leg chelation, sex-modified pleopods, complete mouthparts, internal organs and fluid/feeding functions are not reconstructed. The model is still partly stylized and does not represent every posture, stage or phenotype.

## panulirus-versicolor

A separately proportioned spiny carapace, four separated principal antennular-plate spines, longer antennules, strong tapered second antennae, smooth six-somite banded abdomen with shallow selected pubescent-area cues on somites2/3, five-part fan and ten articulated non-chelate limbs replace the generic draft.

An original irregular blue-green and pale cuticle mosaic, pale abdominal bands with dark margins, longitudinal pale/dark leg stripes, selected pink basal antennae with pale flagella, and original fan-ray cues follow the retained OpenCage photograph and scoped FAO descriptions.

The same retained surfaces perform a short supported forward bout, antenna/antennule articulation, partial search gesture, quiet support and mild serial tail/fan flex. No great Homarus claws, measured locomotor sequence, escape displacement, food interaction, stridulation or physiological sensing is claimed.

Evidence: The authentic OpenCage CC BY-SA2.5 photograph and selected actual Naturalis/FAO and Smithsonian SERC account diagnoses were compared. Actual FAO1998 PDFpage48 printed1013 Fig20f and page56 printed1021 species illustration/diagnosis were rendered/viewed. Smithsonian account morphology is distinguished from a collection determination; the photograph comes from OpenCage, not SERC. Shallow pubescent areas are distinguished from transverse grooves, and adult/juvenile color descriptions are scoped.

Remaining: Exact body ratios, principal/secondary spine positions, pubescent fields and setae, fan/pleopod membranes, antennal articulation and mouthparts remain approximate. Original pigment and roughness are not measured cuticle optics or a scan. Source-photo stage/sex are not independently redetermined. Female P5 chelation, internal anatomy, true sensory processing, stridulation, food handling/ingestion and hydrodynamic escape are absent. The model remains partly stylized, with authored unmeasured support and partial tail articulation rather than validated biological biomechanics.
