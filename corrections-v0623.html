Pinus sylvestris

Original irregular crown, continuous tapered trunk and branched woody axes with individually modeled paired needle cues and selected pendent woody seed cones; foliage and cone details can be inspected through anatomy cards.

Original continuous fissured grey-brown lower bark, warmer upper bark, blue-green paired needle surfaces and muted woody cone-scale pigmentation; no photographic pixels or measured tissue reflectance.

Remaining: The selected crown is still more open and regular than many mature individuals. Needle density, bark relief, fine scale construction and natural optical response remain simplified; no measured proportions, age, environmental response or complete seedling development.

Pinus pinea

Original radiating umbrella-like crown and tapered woody axes with individually modeled long paired needles and broad selected seed cones. Juvenile solitary primary needles are distinct from these adult secondary needle cues.

Original darker fissured bark, longer paired green needle surfaces and broad brown woody scale forms. Pigment, sheen and relief are authored rather than fitted to measured optics.

Remaining: The umbrella crown remains a selected simplified form, not a measured mature specimen. Cone inspection is partly obscured by retained needles; scale apophyses and seed cavities remain coarse. Bark relief, needle density, tissue optics, age variation and primary-to-secondary needle development remain incomplete.

Picea abies

Original conical branching hierarchy with drooping fine shoots, individual single pointed needle surfaces with partial brown insertion cues, scaly bark pigmentation and selected elongated pendent seed cones.

Original dark-green single pointed quadrangular needle cues, partial brown basal insertion cues, muted scaly-bark pigment and thin woody cone-scale studies. Natural wax, stomata and optical response are not calibrated.

Remaining: The conical crown and branch hierarchy remain too regular in places. Some lower diagnostic views obscure the cone behind retained shoots; sterigmata, scale margins, needle epidermis and natural optics are incomplete. No measured wind mechanics, age-specific allometry, full seed development or cone-release sequence.

Abies alba

Original fir branching hierarchy, selected two-ranked flattened needle cues with pale lower bands, greyer continuous bark and upright narrow bracted cone studies. Cone-bearing upper shoot cues are more ascending; foliage arrangement varies with shoot context.

Original grey continuous bark, flattened darker green needle surfaces with pale lower-band cues and upright bracted woody cone studies. These are original numerical surfaces, not calibrated photographic textures.

Remaining: The whole crown retains conspicuous regular tiering and bright needle-edge aliasing in some views. Fine leaf tips, stomatal bands, bark relief and bract/scale anatomy remain idealized. Cone disintegration, seed release, measured foliage optics and a complete seed-to-tree developmental sequence remain unavailable.
