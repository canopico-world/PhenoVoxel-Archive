Exterior detail with documented provenance.

Green sea turtle

DigitalLife3D’s juvenile Chelonia mydas replaces the earlier generic artist body. The source was reconstructed from photographs and measurements of a live juvenile by Johnson Martin, with Inwater Research Group. The creator reports a straight minimum carapace length of 27.5 cm and mass of 3.0 kg. Those measurements describe the source individual; the viewer uses a normalized display scale.

Source image dimensions up to 4096 × 4096 are retained, including shell and skin pattern, eyes and normal detail. The original weighted body and Swim Cycle remain together. This juvenile is not the individual in the separately credited reference photograph, and its phenotype is not inferred from the DNA record.

Axolotl

The Animal Animator model supplies detailed skin, a finned tail, three external gill rami per side, four fore digits and five hind digits. Its body maps reach 4096 × 4096 and gill map 2048 × 2048. Original walking, swimming, feeding and quiet-movement clips share one creator skeleton and surface.

The displayed form is leucistic and captive-type. It is not the typical dark wild pigmentation shown in the archive photograph. This is an artist reconstruction rather than a measured specimen scan. Visible gills, skin and jaw movement do not calculate oxygen exchange, muscle forces, suction pressure or regeneration.

Red-eyed tree frog

MadeByYeshe’s original GLB retains its textured green skin, colored flanks, red eyes, toe pads, original skeleton and four authored clips. The file is retained byte for byte. The viewer plays the original Hop Cycle and idle clips; it does not remodel, repaint, retime their internal keyframes or combine them into a new animation.

Camera orbit, display scale, clip selection and seeking are viewing controls. The GLB export returns the creator’s original file and animations. A modified posed model is not exported under this source’s no-derivatives license. Anatomy, adhesion, jump forces and physiological sleep remain unvalidated artist representations.

Movement studies and physiology context.

Turtle propulsion

Swim plays the original creator cycle. Glide holds one source pose; Slow swim approach is explicitly 0.55× viewing playback, with no vegetation contact or ingestion. Quiet inspection holds a pose rather than simulating sleep. A 2022 primary study of wild green turtles reports a five-stage sweep stroke and differences from captive juveniles. The juvenile creator clip is not presented as the measured wild-adult trajectory. Lungs, dive physiology, buoyancy and hydrodynamic forces are not solved.

Axolotl locomotion and feeding

D’Août and Aerts (1997) document anguilliform swimming in adult axolotls. The source swim clip illustrates lateral trunk/tail motion; Top view makes that plane easier to inspect. Bottom walk, feeding motion and quiet movement use separate original creator clips. Timing, wavelength and gill movement are authored. Jaw/gular movement does not calculate suction pressure, prey capture or gas exchange.

Frog hop and quiet postures

Hop Cycle remains the original creator animation. Look around uses Idle 3; Quiet posture uses Idle 2. These are source-motion studies, not calibrated foraging, leaf adhesion or sleep. Smithsonian keeper observations support nocturnal activity, adhesive toe pads and resting eye protection. Biology cards link to written context separately from what the model demonstrates.

All three include a Skin & head detail camera, side/opposite/front/top views and an underside view. The same body, UVs and materials remain through actions. Approximate surface labels follow the source skin. No skin micrograph, complete organ reconstruction, experimental movement recording or independent anatomical certification is claimed.

The frog record DQ283423.1 is not a complete mitochondrial genome. Its single deposited annotation groups 12S ribosomal RNA, tRNA-Val and 16S ribosomal RNA without separate boundaries. The interface therefore shows one source-annotated region, fragment start/end labels and no protein translation. It does not invent a gene map or a circular origin for the fragment.

The turtle and axolotl records reproduce all 26 deposited CDS translations using vertebrate mitochondrial genetic code 2. Together with the earlier two mammals, the lab now has five species and 52 reproducible deposited protein translations. Real source strand, coding frame and stop-codon annotations remain available. Nuclear genomes, reads, coverage and population-wide variation are not loaded.
