Research desk · validation, uncertainty and limitations

All 1,519 deposited trials in Source Data 1, sheet 1e, are retained. Each trial is baseline-corrected over the first 120 samples, averaged within fly and condition, then flies receive equal weight. The first 420 of 421 samples form −2 to <5 s at 60 Hz; no extra smoothing is applied.

95% intervals describe uncertainty in the across-fly mean using 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of 17 flies. They are not individual prediction intervals or confidence in what an animal sees. Repeated trials are not independent animals.

Each held-out prediction excludes that fly from every training condition. This is a post-hoc check within one dataset, not prospective or cross-laboratory validation. The additive hypothesis is documented in the original paper; the condition-mean benchmark and intervals here are PhenoVoxel secondary analyses.

The source includes male Canton-S flies; experiments used 2–6-day-old males raised at 18 °C, a freely walking arena and ~520 nm projected light. These are different individuals from the anatomical reconstructions. Only deposited pattern classes are selectable; missing trial-specific speed/contrast metadata prevent an arbitrary stimulus tuning model.

Our all-17-fly analysis is separate from the paper’s aggregate panel: the author plotting code applies additional trial-count selection. The original aggregate curves are retained in the downloadable data for comparison.

Six cells come from MaleCNS v1.0, an adult male reconstruction. Source SWC centerlines are displayed without losing nodes; 8 nm coordinate units are converted to micrometres. The annotations include preliminary tracing labels, shown for every cell.

For each selected descending neuron, consider two-connection paths through annotated nerve-cord interneurons to T1 motor neurons with at least 10 source synapses on each edge. Show the path maximizing the smaller edge count; ties use first count, second count, intermediary ID and endpoint ID, descending. REAL DEVICE / LOCAL REPORT

How does it run on your phone?

Run this page on your actual iPhone or iPad. A simulated viewport does not test the device. The report stays local until you choose to share it.

  1. First, open the whole-body fly study in another tab. Check loading, orbit/zoom, walking and flight playback. Return here and record those observations below.
  2. Reload this exhibit once. Check that its controls fit.
  3. Play, pause and reset the experiment. Rotate the phone to landscape and back.
  4. Open motor pathways. Rotate and pinch-zoom the skeleton; test the + / − buttons. Page zoom remains available outside the canvas.
  5. Background Safari, then return. Playback should stay paused.
  6. Run the three-minute anatomy session below. Note warmth, unexpected dimming, stuttering or a reload.

No physical-device result has been recorded.

This is a finite 30-frame/s-target anatomy workload, not a thermal stress test or a full-archive performance certification. Stop whenever needed. The report contains frame intervals, draw durations, viewport, loading timings and your observations. Whole-body checks are manual and do not certify every archive species. Temperature is not measured, and a frame-rate drop does not establish thermal throttling. Browser and network caches affect loading times. No automatic upload, camera or microphone access.
