Cheetah — individual review

Initial author comparison of an unmeasured, partly stylized artist reconstruction. Not a 90% accuracy score or independent scientific validation. A coherent initial correction, not completion of the 50-animal request or of all archive movement/development work.

Identity and evidence

Acinonyx jubatus is one accepted species concept in the checked MDD/GBIF records. The Smithsonian’s species account supports the common name Cheetah. No Smithsonian collection determination was obtained. Photograph observations include subspecies labels; these are not independently determined or counted separately.

Body

A distinct slender, solid-spotted cheetah artist body replaces no existing species. The 25-joint runtime hierarchy articulates pelvis, lumbar/thoracic regions, neck/head, tail and four three-segment limbs. The source tail/neck proportions, hip volumes, face and toe anatomy remain unmeasured and partly stylized.

Surface

Retained original UV atlas and source image bytes. Short tapered hairs inherit the same surface colors and skin weights. Eighteen separate oversized hooked white claws were shortened and toned down at runtime using author-selected factors, guided by authentic photographs and the Smithsonian description of blunt semi-retractile claws. No photographic pixels were painted into invented anatomy. Exact fur optics, individual markings and claw dimensions remain unmeasured.

Movement

A finite lateral four-foot stepping study has independently grounded stance/recovery intervals, forward trunk progression, modest authored trunk lowering, recovery paw pitch and subtle spine/tail articulation. It stops after 4.2 display seconds. Source-pose fitting, higher carpal landmarks and continuous chain-coordinate skin weights replaced an initially overconstrained/folding wrist solution. A separate bounded head turn returns to the original pose. Gallop, prey handling, repose and sleep remain unavailable.

Day and Jayne’s primary study includes three cheetahs among 25 captive felids. Selected methods and the actual lateral cheetah joint diagram/Table 3 on printed p. 645 were read and rendered. Lateral walking and joint conventions guide the study; no source measurements or trajectories were fitted. Official text was accessible, but the locally rendered figure came from a paper mirror after the official PDF download failed. The paper is cited, not redistributed.

Changes

  • Retained hendrikReyneke’s credited cheetah surface and atlas, with unchanged source/delivery GLB bytes.
  • Added connected-limb segmentation so adjacent right fore/hind paws do not inherit each other’s weights.
  • Authored a 25-joint pelvis/spine/head/tail/four-limb hierarchy and bounded lateral stepping with separate support and recovery.
  • Corrected shoulder/carpal placement, reachable support targets and paw orientation after failed support tests and actual screenshots.
  • Replaced discontinuous height-based skinning with continuous chain-coordinate blending after a close-up revealed an open-looking carpal crease.
  • Shortened 18 exaggerated hooked claws and added a short-fur surface derivative; original source file retained.
  • Added authentic walking, face and lateral-body photographs with exact creator, license, source and actual derivative/delivery checksums. Rejected a footprint-only candidate as a body photograph.
  • Preserved all 306 earlier species objects, including the wolf/tiger surfaces and action paths.

Actual final comparisons

All 36 nonblank final frames were inspected individually. The four sheets retain source credits and authentic reference photographs. Rejected draft captures are excluded.

Residual limits and pending mechanisms

  • Exact head/eye/lip, tail/neck proportions, shoulder-blade anatomy, pads and fine digits remain incomplete or unmeasured.
  • Short fur and coat colors are qualitative artist derivatives, not calibrated optical or photo-matched surfaces.
  • Only a bounded walking study and local head turn are implemented; continuous gait, galloping/aerial phases, prey acquisition/ingestion, repose and physiological sleep remain unfinished.
  • No fitted animal trajectories, forces, contact deformation, energetics, respiratory physiology, population-wide dimensions or independent specialist validation are established.
  • This single admission is 5 of 50 requested new large animals after deployment verification; 45 remain. Other incomplete archive mechanisms are not marked complete.

Software review

The final local regression passed 232 software checks, followed by 10 focused checks after readability edits. The initial full pass had 228 passes and two metadata-format failures; those fields were corrected. Software checks do not establish biological accuracy. Build, compiled rendering and publication verification are separate recorded gates.
