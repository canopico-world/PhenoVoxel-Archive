A phone-first foundation for studying organism–environment interactions. The new section sits beneath each model. Its camera transformations are illustrative image operations, not validated reconstructions of animal experience.

Three types of evidence stay separate

Device observation: processed RGB pixels and microphone spectra. Illustrative model: transformations, spectral peak diagrams and signaling pathways. Unmeasured channel: information this device or study has not captured.

No extra hardware is required for these exploratory tools. That does not make a phone a UV camera, hydrophone, chemical sensor or electrophysiology instrument.

What you can do now

  1. Select a species and open Sense & Sensibility beneath its model.
  2. Use a credited reference photograph, test chart, local photo or live rear camera. Compare RGB with achromatic luminance, visible edges or changes between frames. Freeze a frame to release the camera.
  3. Hearing studies remain under maintenance; microphone capture is unavailable until a species-specific interpretation is reviewed.
  4. Explore a finite receptor/signaling pathway and wavelength probe. The photon-energy calculation is E = hc/λ; no atoms or cellular signals are being observed.
  5. Export a labeled PNG or a JSON research note with settings, evidence, source type and limits. The JSON contains no photo, audio or device identifier.

Measurement and model limits

RGB images are processed by the camera and display pipeline. The current calculation assumes sRGB decoding, then uses relative luminance 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B. Mean and RMS contrast describe that image, including any letterbox borders, not scene irradiance, reflectance, lux or receptor catch. Analysis uses a 480 × 300 raster. Spatial reduction is expressed in analysis pixels, not retinal resolution. Motion maps also respond to camera movement, auto exposure and changing light.

Microphone FFT size is 2048. Frequency-bin spacing is sample rate divided by FFT size; display is limited to 30 Hz–min(20 kHz, Nyquist). The −95 dB peak-display cutoff is a software heuristic, not an animal hearing threshold. Digital analyser levels are not calibrated sound pressure. Disabling requested browser audio processing does not guarantee that device processing is absent. Frozen spectra retain the sample rate used at capture.

Photopigment peak diagrams use explicitly schematic Gaussian shapes. They are not measured sensitivity curves or receptor-noise discrimination predictions. Photon energy uses 1239.841984 eV·nm / wavelength. The pathway playback has six illustrative seconds and no biological rate interpretation.

A credible route toward discovery

  1. Build a versioned sensory evidence registry. Record taxon, tissue, age/sex where known, environment, experimental method, sample size, units, uncertainty, source and reuse license. Keep measured functions separate from inferred and unknown parameters.
  2. Model what the input can support. Receptor-catch modeling requires spectral sensitivities and sufficiently characterized scene/camera data. Missing spectra cannot be uniquely recovered from three RGB channels. For ordinary phones, show supported contrasts and uncertainty; reserve UV or full-spectrum comparisons for existing licensed laboratory datasets. Troscianko & Stevens (2015) provides a calibration and analysis foundation.
  3. Test predictions independently. Define a discrimination question before fitting the model. Compare predicted visibility or detection with held-out behavioral responses, across individuals and conditions. Report errors and uncertainty. A successful code test validates a calculation, not biology. Nonverbal choices can test discrimination without proving what an experience feels like.
  4. Use phones for repeatable field observations. Record conditions, distance, lighting and device settings where available. Repeat observations and retain source provenance. Camera timelapse could later quantify visible growth or behavior; it would still not directly establish molecular signaling. This release does not yet implement timelapse or automated organism tracking.
  5. Study communication with context and interventions. Link acoustic events to independently annotated behavior; test generalization to new individuals and settings. Meaning requires stronger evidence than clustering sounds. Sharma et al. (2024) reports contextual/combinatorial sperm-whale coda structure, not a translation dictionary. Specialist-led, ethical experiments would be needed before interpreting a signal as an answer.

Privacy and device requirements

Modern browsers, HTTPS (or localhost), and explicit permission are required for camera/microphone use. Media analysis runs locally; this lab has no media upload endpoint. Closing the lab, changing tabs/species, hiding the document or leaving the page stops its sensors. Late permission responses are canceled. Notes are session-only unless exported. The rest of PhenoVoxel still uses its existing external reference/map services.

Browser permission failures retain the demo/photo route. Real iOS Safari, Android Chrome and iPad hardware capture remain to be validated; a narrow desktop viewport is a layout check, not hardware certification. Embedded browsers may restrict capture or downloads.

Software verification

Twenty-five focused automated checks (including distinct species defaults, colour-proxy channel isolation and reference-photo provenance) cover photon energy, sRGB/contrast operations, temporal and spatial differences (including preservation of frozen motion comparisons), actual audio sample-rate mapping, silent inputs, late/concurrent permission requests, failed-request recovery, evidence scope, fail-closed species/channel access, maintenance markup and notebook provenance. The desktop browser review exercised the demo modes, synthetic spectrum, finite pathway, species switching and local JSON export; responsive layout was inspected at 390 CSS pixels. The existing species database is unchanged.

This is an experimental educational and research-design foundation. No exact animal-vision simulator, communication translator, independent physiological validation or new biological discovery is claimed.

Four additional visual research studies · v0.7.4

Chimpanzee, monarch butterfly, adult zebrafish and green sea turtle now have a separate spectral workbench. Camera displays are uncalibrated visible references (monarch: explicitly incomplete blue/green proxy). They do not invert camera RGB into spectra or reconstruct subjective appearance.

Use synthetic reflectance pairs or import a five-column CSV with wavelength, A/B reflectance, photon illuminant and transmission. The workbench integrates I × R × T × S from 300–750 nm at 5 nm spacing, normalizes each channel to a white reflector, and exports all inputs, curves, assumptions and source links. Govardovskii A1 alpha bands and the Stavenga alpha expression are approximations; beta bands, oil droplets, ocular filters and neural processing are not fitted. Model distance is Euclidean, not a JND or a validated discrimination prediction.

  • Chimpanzee: S/M/L model at 430/531/563 nm, combining ERG evidence with published ecological model choices. Human-like RGB is retained as a reference.
  • Monarch: UV ≤340, blue 435 and green 540 nm. The nominal UV peak is set at the reported upper bound. The three-class model omits filtering implicated in long-wave discrimination and does not simulate polarization.
  • Zebrafish: adult AB-strain fits at 365/416/483/574 nm. A1-only approximations omit appreciable A2 contributions; larval datasets must be treated separately.
  • Green turtle: measured cone pigments at 440/502/562 nm. The optional 365 nm UV channel is labelled as a cross-species assumption from published modelling. Six-month-old hatchling colour-choice results are not generalized to adult subjective vision.

Phone-only users can explore published spectra and record visible observations. New claims require independent age- and condition-matched behavioural validation, control of intensity, held-out samples and appropriate oversight. Missing UV, polarization and calibrated spectral radiance cannot be obtained by adding a filter to ordinary RGB.
