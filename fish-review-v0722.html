Atlantic sturgeon and American eel

Atlantic sturgeon

The restored Blender rig keeps both pectoral fins in their source positions. The original photographic color and surface maps bring out the bony scutes. The browser texture includes the roughness inversion from the Blender material. The underside view shows the barbels and mouth. The viewer includes steady swimming, two turning studies and a faster swimming cycle.

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service anatomy reference · DigitalLife Model 66A

American eel

The restored source keeps the pectoral fins attached behind the gill openings and preserves the continuous dorsal, tail and anal fin. The swimming study follows the lateral wave down the body.

USGS morphology reference · Tytell's American eel swimming study · DigitalLife Model 71A

What was checked

Browser mesh bounds were compared with Blender at six poses per animation. Tests also checked finite playback, seeking, reset and embedded 4K photographic color maps. Both fish use a separate illustrative eye finish. The original creator clips have authored timing and amplitudes, and the photographic reconstruction combines scans with live reference photos. Living reflectance and swimming forces would require independent measurements.
