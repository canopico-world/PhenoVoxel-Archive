Evidence and scope

The Australian Antarctic Program and New Zealand Birds Online inform the emperor adult exterior. The new penguin uses a continuous closed feathered trunk, independent stiff flippers, two webbed feet and a short tail. The damaged museum-scan geometry and previous cut-and-cap repair are no longer used in the active penguin.

Sato et al. (2007) supplies emperor stroke-frequency context and broader penguin stroke mechanics; Williams et al. (2012) supplies emperor stroke and dive context. Paired dorsoventral flipper strokes and trailing feet are illustrated qualitatively. The model's joint angles, 1.25 display cycles per second, eight-second study and body dimensions are authored, not fitted measurements.

Cornell Lab's adult photographs inform the folded-wing eagle exterior. Its standing exterior is the separately credited Grab3D CC0 model, reduced from800k to79,992 triangles with2k texture maps. The detailed Pigcraft flight exterior remains credited. Standing is held; there is no landing, wing-fold transition, head-turn cycle, grasping or balance simulation.

These are exterior reconstructions with illustrative materials, not photographs or independently validated anatomy. Full physiology, oxygen use, metabolism, heat transfer, heart rate, buoyancy, aerodynamic and hydrodynamic forces are not simulated. No numerical accuracy percentage or biological certification is claimed.

Review and preservation

The images are actual model renders. Review includes front, rear, opposite side, top and underside, standing, paired strokes and flight poses. The penguin trunk has a welded closed-surface check, short edge checks and an unchanged rigid trunk through swimming. The eagle standing exterior does not use the previous compression morph. Camera bounds include all displayed poses. The finite penguin walk advances through four short steps, with planted stance feet and a lifted recovery arc; timing and stride dimensions remain authored.
