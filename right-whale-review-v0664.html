North Atlantic and North Pacific right whales

Scientific checks and the reconstruction choices

FeatureEvidence and resulting model
Right-whale body planBoth northern forms have robust dark bodies, finless backs, bowed lips, narrow arched rostra, broad paddle-shaped flippers and broad, notched black flukes. The models retain those shared features. NOAA: Atlantic · NOAA: Pacific · NOAA identification guide.
Pale head padsCallosities are thickened skin; light-coloured cyamid whale lice give them their conspicuous pale appearance. Their patterns identify individuals. The new surfaces use irregular raised patches and fine roughness, with different rostral, chin, lip and eye-region arrangements for the two selected appearances. New England Aquarium: callosities and photo identification.
Differences between the bodiesThe Pacific interpretation has a somewhat fuller mid-body; the Atlantic interpretation has a slightly longer selected head region. These are modest illustration choices within the shared body plan. The reference photographs have perspective and water distortion, so their pixels do not supply calibrated widths. External morphology overlaps among all three right-whale species. COSEWIC: descriptive morphology and overlap.
Skin and markingsIndependent dark-grey pigment fields, subtle irregular wear, separate roughness and normal maps, and restrained wet reflections replace the old simplified surfaces. The Atlantic rostral bands and Pacific bonnet/chin emphasis follow visible regions in the website photos. Unseen sides and undersides are authored. Both models use dark ventral appearances; white belly patches are variable and are not required of every animal.
MovementThe same body and skin remain through the finite local fluke-stroke and turning studies. Posterior bending is dorsoventral and texture-chart normals stay joined. Display timing and excursion are authored. Feeding remains pending; these closed-mouth studies do not simulate filtration, prey capture, flow, force or energetics.

Southern source, credits and limits

Both northern bodies derive from Model 55A — Southern Right Whale, by the Digital Life project, University of Massachusetts Amherst, with CG artist Robert Gutierrez. The source is credited under CC BY-NC 4.0. WHOI documents the Southern model’s research and artist lineage. The northern interpretations do not inherit a northern-specimen measurement claim from that research.

The source pose was baked into a static exterior. The new northern derivatives have selected shape adjustments, independent texture charts, new numeric pigment/normal/roughness maps and local callosity relief. The original Southern atlas, skeleton and creator animation are omitted from the northern assets. Dark eyes and closed-mouth baleen are retained. The existing Southern model, its record and its source file are preserved.

The supplied photo frames are displayed separately with their own credits. Their pixels are not used in the material maps. No image-generation or paid 3D service was used; service credits spent: zero. See the complete model and photograph rights note.

These are reference-guided educational reconstructions. They have not been independently certified as biological replicas. The images here are renders of the actual active models using the compatibility software renderer; WebGL uses the same geometry and material maps. A different phone or lighting environment can change appearance. Exact individual dimensions, tissue optics, callosity identity and movement timing remain unmeasured.
