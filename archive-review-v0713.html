PHENOVOXEL RESEARCH · v0.7.13 · 11 NOVEMBER 2026

Explore places, relatives and ten ways of sensing light.

Two compact explorers

  • Biogeography: one bounded world map with country outlines, country/year filters, density legend, live totals and matching GBIF record links. Region buttons move the view; only country/year controls filter records.
  • Tree of Life: a collapsible connection view starts at a shared archived group. Numbered nodes match branch cards. Rotate, climb to broader groups, reset or jump to a relative.

GBIF occurrences are reported records, not animal counts, native ranges or absence evidence. Natural Earth boundaries are public-domain cartographic context. The lineage view is a classification navigator, not a dated molecular phylogeny; shared groups do not establish nearest living relatives.

Evidence and hypotheses remain separate

These ten studies use published species evidence to motivate interactive calculations. They are not biological experiments, new peer-reviewed discoveries or validated reproductions of subjective vision. Scenario sliders are not 95% confidence intervals. No fitted perceptual dataset supports such intervals here; exports explicitly use a null perceptual interval.

A normal RGB phone camera cannot measure UV, polarization, retinal signals or calibrated animal irradiance. The prawn and whale-shark calculations preserve the RGB image because the required physical information is missing. Non-imaging pathway demonstrations use scalar signals instead of invented scenes. Pond snails also have eyes: the new study isolates their dermal shadow pathway.

Licensed habitat photographs are human-view context. They do not specify an animal's gaze, viewing height, visual field or spectral sensitivity. Camera and notebook exports retain their existing privacy controls.

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Emperor penguin ↗

A diamond pupil changes admitted light

Published evidence: Strongly constricted emperor-penguin pupils were diamond shaped in a comparative optical study.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: For an ideal diamond, area = width × height / 2. Relative area is the product of the two diagonal ratios. Scale linear RGB by that product as an exposure illustration; no diamond-shaped field mask is imposed.

Limits: Assigned dimensions, fixed irradiance and no adaptation. Pupil shape does not make the scene diamond-shaped. No fitted color perception, underwater optics or low-light threshold.

Validation needed: Fit species-specific pupil geometry and irradiance measurements; validate separately in air and water.

Habitat context: Antarctic pack ice. Jason Auch · CC BY 2.0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Lind, Kelber & Kröger (2008) · Multifocal optical systems and pupil dynamics in birds

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Whale shark ↗

A blue-shifted rod pigment

Published evidence: Reconstituted whale-shark rhodopsin peaks at 478 nm; molecular experiments link substitutions to spectral tuning and stability.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Calculate an A1 alpha-band template at nominal peak 478 nm. Keep the photograph unchanged: RGB cannot determine per-pixel spectra or receptor catches.

Limits: In-vitro peak is not a color percept. No fitted thermal kinetics, pigment concentration, cone signal, depth or water attenuation.

Validation needed: Measure ocular transmission and calibrated spectral scenes; test predictions with independent live-animal responses.

Habitat context: Maldives reef passage. Bruno de Giusti · CC BY-SA 2.5 it. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Yamaguchi et al. (2023) · Whale shark rhodopsin and deep-sea adaptation · Govardovskii et al. (2000) · Visual pigment templates

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

White shark ↗

Contrast after chromatic information is removed

Published evidence: Retinal opsin evidence supports cone monochromacy in lamniform sharks, including white shark.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Interpolate linear RGB toward human-weighted luminance. Default removes chromatic cues as a hypothesis, not a measured white-shark transfer function.

Limits: Rod–cone interactions, adaptation, motion and neural weighting are omitted. Grayscale is a display convention, not proof of subjective black-and-white experience.

Validation needed: Use retinal spectra and intensity-controlled discrimination trials; validate recognition independently.

Habitat context: Rocky kelp habitat. Shane Anderson / NOAA · Public domain. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Hart et al. (2025) · Cone monochromacy in galeomorph sharks

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Atlantic ghost crab ↗

An acute zone in a compound eye

Published evidence: Ghost-crab eye morphology shows an anterior acute zone; ERG experiments found a single 494 nm sensitivity peak.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Keep an assigned central disk detailed; pool linear RGB outside it. The disk is an explanatory mask, not a fitted map of individual ommatidia.

Limits: ERG peak is not a complete color transform. Radius, center and pooling are assumptions; no panoramic field, acceptance angles or detection thresholds are reconstructed.

Validation needed: Fit eye geometry and receptor fields, then test independent target-detection tasks.

Habitat context: Florida coastal dunes. Balon Greyjoy · CC0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Qian & Frank (2024) · Comparative visual physiology of Ocypode and Aratus

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Common prawn ↗

A visual cue can exist without driving gaze

Published evidence: Pooled Palaemon shrimp responded to intensity gratings but not polarization-only gratings in a gaze-stabilization assay.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: For hypothetical orthogonal ideal analyzers, I0 = (1 + p cos 2θ)/2 and I90 = (1 − p cos 2θ)/2. Calculate their difference. Keep habitat RGB unchanged.

Limits: Phone RGB does not measure polarization. Analyzer channels are physics inputs, not fitted prawn receptors or predicted gaze; negative assay results do not exclude other polarization tasks.

Validation needed: Use species-separated trials, matched intensity and measured Stokes inputs; test behavior independently.

Habitat context: Scottish kelp habitat. David Baird · CC BY-SA 2.0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Drerup & How (2021) · Polarization contrasts and crustacean gaze stabilization

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Red kangaroo ↗

From opsin sequence to an untested color hypothesis

Published evidence: A marsupial genome comparison includes red kangaroo and proposes Macropus-lineage visual adaptations from sequence changes.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Remove a chosen fraction of linear-RGB chromatic differences. This deliberately tests information loss without inventing measured kangaroo cone peaks.

Limits: Genomic inference is not a behavioral color test. This is neither a dichromat simulator nor a calibrated kangaroo percept; UV and twilight performance remain unmeasured.

Validation needed: Establish pigment spectra, ocular transmission and brightness-controlled behavior in this species.

Habitat context: Australian arid scrubland. Derek Barry · CC BY 2.5. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Molecular evolution of vision-related genes in marsupials (2022)

VISION / OPTICS HYPOTHESIS

Common starfish ↗

Separate directional information from a stitched view

Published evidence: Opsin studies distinguish optic cushions from putative extraocular photosensory tissue in Asterias rubens.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Average luminance in user-selected vertical sectors. Five is an illustrative starting value, not one camera sector per arm or a measured retinal sampling count.

Limits: No stitched five-eye percept, spectral sensitivity or acuity is fitted. Body orientation and extraocular sensing are omitted; this is an information diagram.

Validation needed: Measure each eye’s field and response, then test directional behavior independently.

Habitat context: Scottish kelp habitat. David Baird · CC BY-SA 2.0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Delroisse et al. (2013) · Opsin detection in Asterias rubens and Paracentrotus

NON-IMAGING PATHWAY

Freshwater hydra ↗

Light can modulate a stinging-cell pathway

Published evidence: Bright light reduced stinging-cell discharge in experiments published as H. magnipapillata, now H. vulgaris.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Illustrative gate = 1/(1 + coefficient × illumination). The uniform display shows the assigned gate, not a camera picture or fitted firing probability.

Limits: Equation is an authored hypothesis; no estimated coefficients, receptor activation or firing rates. Mechanical and chemical cues and strain variation are omitted.

Validation needed: Fit trial-level light/mechanical/chemical experiments, then test a held-out strain and exposure range.

Habitat context: Submerged pond plants. Reto Gerber · CC0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Plachetzki, Fong & Oakley (2012) · Light regulation of cnidocyte discharge · NHGRI Hydra genome project · strain and name history

NON-IMAGING PATHWAY

Great pond snail ↗

A shadow sensed through the mantle

Published evidence: Mantle photoreceptors participate in light-off responses connected to withdrawal. Lymnaea also has eyes; this study isolates the dermal pathway.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Compute max(0, earlier − later) and a relative decrease when earlier is nonzero. Display the absolute decrease as a uniform signal; no flicker is emitted.

Limits: Levels are supplied assumptions, not calibrated camera irradiance. No neural delay, habituation or withdrawal probability is fitted; not a reconstruction of ocular vision.

Validation needed: Fit irradiance-step recordings with individual and adaptation effects; test independent time series.

Habitat context: Submerged pond plants. Reto Gerber · CC0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Takigami et al. (2014) · Dermal photoreceptors in Lymnaea stagnalis · Functional characterization of optic photoreception in Lymnaea stagnalis (2024)

NON-IMAGING PATHWAY

Common earthworm ↗

Exposure without an image-forming eye

Published evidence: A 2025 experiment found reduced nighttime foraging at 10 lx and above under controlled-room conditions; field outcomes differed.

PhenoVoxel hypothesis: Compute lux × hours as a descriptive exposure integral. A uniform field shows assigned lux normalized to the control range; it is not the worm’s brightness experience.

Limits: Lux weights human photopic sensitivity. No camera lux measurement, biological threshold, reciprocity law or behavioral dose-response is assumed. Spectrum, moisture, duration and context matter.

Validation needed: Use measured spectral irradiance, exposure history and independent behavioral datasets; compare field and laboratory settings.

Habitat context: Leaf litter and shaded ground. Z3lvs · CC0. Human-camera context, not recorded animal sensation.

Cai, Bennie & Gaston (2025) · Earthworm surface behavior under artificial light at night

Research protocol: confirm species and assay scope; separate observations from assigned parameters; test arithmetic, image preservation and invalid-input handling; require independent biological validation before claiming predictive perception.
