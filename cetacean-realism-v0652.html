Replacing five cartoon-like cetacean reconstructions

The human rejected the v0.6.51 whales and dolphins as cartoon-like. Passing software checks did not establish realism. This release replaces five existing bodies with distinct credited sculpted surfaces and original texture atlases, with bounded same-surface posture/motion studies. It is a partial correction: 24 other newly built species (25 forms) still need deeper reconstruction. It is not completion of the request, photorealism or independent biological validation.

The blue-whale and humpback models and anterior feeding corrections are preserved. No species is added: 337 actual release records. Scheduled work stays off; the human directs subsequent work.

What the sources support

CARI’MAM/Agoa describes educational models made by Weeteam with regional scientific collaboration, including 3D-printing uses. That is source provenance, not independent validation of this archive or a measured motion dataset. Digital Life reports a Southern right-whale ellipsoid reconstructed from drone widths/heights and anatomical reference sculpting. Its source animation remains authored. Neither source is a full living-body scan or proof of a universal body shape.

All five retained whole authentic photographic frames were compared. Sperm/pilot frames expose more of the living exterior; the right-whale breach and rare Kogia/Cuvier surface photographs cannot validate concealed surfaces or fitted kinematics. Existing actual FAO plate comparisons and scoped primary locomotion studies supply separate anatomical/motion context; historical taxonomy is reconciled with modern SMM/GBIF records. No paper figure, private footage, user photo or organism photograph is projected into these artist textures.

Rendered evidence and limits

64 actual final compiled states individually inspected. The final compiled review used software compatibility lighting; no final WebGL optical review is claimed. Original atlases have visible resolution and baked-lighting limits. Same geometry, UVs and colour remain through actions. The four static sources receive selected continuous posterior bending; the Southern right whale retains its source skeleton and one finite five-second creator excerpt at half speed. Local gestures are not complete swimming, feeding, echolocation, flow, force or physiological simulation.

Credited model-only PNG archive · View ledger and actual hashes. Public byte/CI verification is recorded separately after publication.

Rejected and repaired drafts

A rare two-tusk narwhal and a scanned 1993 gray-whale clay sculpture/stand were inspected in three axes and not adopted as ready realistic living bodies. Other restricted, related-taxon or unsuitable sources were not substituted. An initial Southern right-whale material rendered white because the obsolete specular/glossiness extension was unsupported; a JSON-only material conversion restores its original diffuse image, with original binary payload unchanged. The sperm source’s steep tail-down pose was relaxed through a disclosed caudal rotation. The initial pilot saddle was too bright and head-detail framing too tight; runtime dorsal vertex colour and a wider anatomical view corrected those drafts. They are excluded from final accepted evidence.

Software verification

The first full local run passed 357 of 358 checks. One old contract assumed all bundled models used CC BY 4.0; it was narrowed to preserve the original 28 surfaces and separately check the exact noncommercial grants of the five new sources. A duplicated historical-ledger helper introduced during report preparation was removed and its generator made repeatable before the cetacean test files ran again. These are software and provenance checks, not biological validation.

A second full run had already loaded the duplicated helper before its correction and consequently failed that test file. The repaired complete cetacean suites then passed all 11 checks; a final full run follows the repair. Earlier failed logs are retained.

The corrected complete local suite passed 358 of 358 checks. Published CI and live-site checks are recorded separately after deployment.

Sperm whale Physeter macrocephalus

Open corrected study

Changes. Replaced the smooth ring-loft body with the distinct CARI’MAM/Weeteam sculpt and original image atlas. Blunt head, small underslung lower jaw, lateral eyes, wrinkled exterior, paddle flippers, dorsal hump/knuckles and swept flukes now belong to one sculpted surface. Comparison. The whole Vitaly Sokol photograph shows multiple live sperm whales, with light, water and perspective effects. It supports qualitative head, flank and swimming-posture comparison, not measurements or a texture transfer.

Remaining. Source head proportions, jaw closure, flipper attachment and wrinkles are unmeasured. The atlas remains only1024px, with visible resolution and baked-lighting limits close up. The selected tail correction and local bending are not a fitted skeleton or tissue model.

Southern right whale Eubalaena australis

Open corrected study

Changes. Replaced the generic arched body with Digital Life’s distinct Southern right-whale sculpt, original atlas and retained skeleton/source clip. The source geometry has a continuous rostrum/jaw body, paddle flippers, broad flukes and individual callosity patterns. Legacy material compatibility restores the source diffuse maps; source image bytes and binary geometry/rig/animation are unchanged.

Comparison. The whole Hugo Hulsberg breach photograph supports external head/callosity and gross-body comparison but obscures the submerged body. Separate Digital Life provenance reports drone widths/heights at5% length increments plus anatomical sculpting. No North Atlantic or North Pacific right-whale source is substituted.

Remaining. Callosities and scratches remain an artist pattern rather than a redetermined animal; some pale patches are flat and smooth. Creator drone-derived proportions are source-reported, not a new archive morphometric fit. Tissue optics, full filtration and measured swimming remain unresolved.

Pygmy sperm whale Kogia breviceps

Open corrected study

Changes. Replaced the generic rounded template with the distinct CARI’MAM pygmy sperm-whale sculpt and original atlas: blunt shark-like head, small underslung jaw region, compact flippers, low posterior fin, false-gill pigment and a textured countershaded skin.

Comparison. The retained whole Charlotte Kirchner live image is restricted surface evidence. Separate FAO and NOAA species anatomy distinguishes the low posterior fin and false-gill mark from true gills and from the taller central-fin dwarf sperm whale. The dwarf model is not replaced by this K.breviceps body.

Remaining. False-gill detail, lower-jaw margins, eyes, surface grain, source colour and proportions remain selected artist features. The rare live profile photograph shows only the surfaced back/head; it cannot validate the concealed appendages. Suction feeding, full jaw anatomy, discharge and physiology remain unfinished.

Cuvier's beaked whale Ziphius cavirostris

Open corrected study

Changes. Replaced the generic beaked template with CARI’MAM’s distinct Cuvier sculpt and original scarred atlas: goose-like short beak/forehead, pectoral recess regions, compact flippers, posterior fin and selected swept flukes. Display orientation is corrected to the other main-viewer bodies.

Comparison. The retained whole Charlotte Kirchner surface photo shows a brown individual and limited back/head. It is not a same-individual fit to the artist’s pale scarred exterior. NOAA and FAO descriptions provide separate short-beak/concave-forehead and fin context.

Remaining. Pale head and scratches do not independently establish sex/age. Separate erupted apical teeth are not resolved in this artist model, and the record now states that limit. The selected source flukes have a shallow medial indentation. Some scars remain conspicuous/baked; cross-sections, wet optics, suction mechanics and full deep-dive motion are unmeasured.
