Reproducible results

Color sets differ; this is not a causal UV estimate or a reproduction of the original mixed-effects model.

Clownfish lens mean T50: 334.333 nm; 95% t interval 307.771 to 360.895 nm, n=3, df=2. Strong normality and independence assumptions; not an individual prediction interval.

Japanese mantis shrimp

Polarization as a separate physical cue

Evidence. Species-specific genome and light-exposure studies motivate a polarization hypothesis. They do not establish a 12-channel color system.

Hypothesis. An ideal pair of orthogonal analyzers could distinguish some equal-brightness targets with different polarization.

Limits. Analyzer gain and orientation are unmeasured assumptions. Circular polarization and exact retinal organization are not modeled. No polarization is extracted from your camera.

Validation needed. Compare model predictions with independent, brightness-controlled behavioral trials and retinal measurements; gene expression alone cannot validate them.

Oratosquilla chromosome-level genome (2025) · Qu et al. (2024) · Oratosquilla response to linearly polarized light

Common octopus

Equal brightness, different polarization

Evidence. Published training experiments included O. vulgaris discriminating polarization patterns. Table 1 has repeated trials in three animals.

Hypothesis. Changing analyzer orientation should change contrast, even when total light remains equal.

Limits. Ideal analyzers omit retinal irregularity, eye movements and alternative intensity cues. The model is not a reconstruction of subjective appearance. Pooled repeated choices cannot supply an animal-level 95% interval.

Validation needed. Test predictions against held-out individuals while controlling intensity, e-vector orientation and animal posture.

Shashar & Cronin (1996) · Polarization contrast vision in octopus

Barn owl

Angular detail under stated lighting

Evidence. Two owls identified as T. alba pratincola resolved gratings at 2.6–4.0 cycles/degree at 2.7 cd/m².

Hypothesis. At fixed viewing distance, the smallest resolvable grating period scales with angular acuity.

Limits. This is a grating-geometry calculation, not object recognition or a night-vision filter. T. alba pratincola is placed in T. furcata in some taxonomies; transfer to this archive specimen is explicitly uncertain. Two animals do not establish a species-wide interval.

Validation needed. Validate at matched luminance and contrast, in the relevant taxon. Phone auto exposure cannot measure the required luminance.

Harmening et al. (2009) · Barn owl spatial contrast and acuity

Red fox

Two pigment channels, uncertain perception

Evidence. ERG measurements included red foxes and support a short- and a long-wave cone mechanism; 432.5 nm is the midpoint of the reported approximate 430–435 nm range.

Hypothesis. Equal-photon stimuli that separate these channels could supply chromatic information.

Limits. Nominal peak templates omit ocular filters, adaptation, neural processing and receptor noise. Equal photons are assumed. Responses are not discrimination probabilities or perceived colors. UV cannot be recovered from ordinary RGB. Canid behavior is not automatically transferable between species.

Validation needed. Use brightness-controlled red-fox discrimination data to test channel predictions; do not equate dog color naming with fox perception.

Jacobs et al. (1993) · Photopigments of dogs and foxes

Tokay gecko

Ultraviolet in a nocturnal visual system

Evidence. Microspectrophotometry identified three pigment classes, including a 364 nm UV pigment.

Hypothesis. UV differences invisible to a normal phone could change receptor excitation.

Limits. Nominal peak templates omit ocular filters, adaptation, neural processing and receptor noise. Equal photons are assumed. Responses are not discrimination probabilities or perceived colors. UV cannot be recovered from ordinary RGB. Sensitivity under dim light depends on photon noise; no night-vision performance is inferred.

Validation needed. Measure ocular transmission and behavioral discrimination at known illumination before fitting a perceptual model.

Loew (1994) · A third pigment in the Tokay gecko

Common clownfish

The UV channel a phone cannot record

Evidence. Species-specific MSP and controlled behavior support UV contribution to color discrimination. Three individual lens half-transmission wavelengths permit a small-sample reanalysis.

Hypothesis. A UV change can alter nominal channel excitation despite an unchanged visible photograph.

Limits. Published fitted cone curves replace generic templates for this species, including the source UV curve. Synthetic equal-photon illumination and equal gains are assumed. Ocular transmission and the full receptor-noise model are not fitted here; response differences are not discrimination probabilities. Phone UV remains unmeasured.

Validation needed. Replace simplified templates with measured spectra, then validate predictions on held-out fish and brightness-controlled targets.

Mitchell et al. (2024) · UV vision in anemonefish · Mitchell et al. · Open anemonefish data (CC0)

Painted turtle

Behavioral sensitivity is not a pigment peak

Evidence. Photopic flicker photometry in C. picta picta placed the overall sensitivity maximum in 625–640 nm, with evidence for multiple spectral mechanisms.

Hypothesis. A candidate long-wave stimulus can be located relative to this reported maximum region.

Limits. The published maximum region is not a 95% confidence interval. No full action curve, UV channel or photopigment function is reconstructed from its endpoints. Subspecies and adaptation conditions matter.

Validation needed. Recover the measured action spectrum and stimulus conditions before fitting response amplitudes; compare independent wavelength-discrimination data.

Graf (1967) · Painted turtle wavelength discrimination

Common shore crab

Compare one- and two-channel explanations

Evidence. Older MSP sampled a pigment near 502–506 nm. A 2026 MScR thesis reports blue/green discrimination in looming tasks and discusses approximately 440/508 nm channels.

Hypothesis. A two-channel model predicts differential excitation absent from a one-channel explanation.

Limits. Nominal peak templates omit ocular filters, adaptation, neural processing and receptor noise. Equal photons are assumed. Responses are not discrimination probabilities or perceived colors. UV cannot be recovered from ordinary RGB. The 440/508 nm pair is a candidate from the thesis context. The one-channel comparison uses 504 nm, the midpoint of older MSP values. Neither model is a complete crab percept; thesis status is explicit.

Validation needed. Independently replicate brightness-controlled looming and non-looming tasks; measure retinal channels and compare models on held-out trials.

Bruno, Mote & Goldsmith (1973) · Green crab single ommatidia · Alferoff (2026) · Shore crab color vision, MScR thesis

Large water flea

Four measured spectral classes

Evidence. Chromatic adaptation resolved four classes in single ommatidia; these nominal peaks are from the dorsal region.

Hypothesis. A short-wave stimulus can excite a channel absent from a three-channel visible camera.

Limits. Nominal peak templates omit ocular filters, adaptation, neural processing and receptor noise. Equal photons are assumed. Responses are not discrimination probabilities or perceived colors. UV cannot be recovered from ordinary RGB. Dorsal values are not a universal whole-eye fit. Four spectral classes do not by themselves prove four-dimensional color behavior.

Validation needed. Compare adapted dorsal and ventral measurements, and independently test wavelength discrimination at matched brightness.

Smith & Macagno (1990) · Daphnia UV photoreceptors

C. elegans

Spectral cues without an image-forming eye

Evidence. Blue:amber light and aversive chemical context affected foraging. Across 59 wild strains, the reported correlation between condition-dependent responses was r=.24.

Hypothesis. Changing spectral balance alone is insufficient to predict avoidance across strains and chemical environments.

Limits. No camera view represents a worm’s visual scene. Photon fractions below are inputs, not a fitted avoidance probability. The correlation interval uses a rounded summary and assumes independent strains; relatedness is not modeled.

Validation needed. Use strain-resolved independent assays, explicitly model relatedness and chemical context, and validate on held-out strains.

Ghosh et al. (2021) · C. elegans color-dependent foraging

Methods audit and PhenoVoxel remarks

  • Resample 11 fish, not 49 threshold rows or thousands of choices. Neutral rows excluded from the paired UV-positive versus UV-negative comparison.
  • Bootstrap treats published threshold estimates as fixed. Color-set, order, learning, fitted-threshold error and receptor-noise uncertainty remain unresolved.
  • Clownfish uses the authors’ published four-cone fitted curves, including the A2-based UV fit. Neural noise, ocular filtering and threshold fitting are not reproduced.
  • Lens t interval describes a population mean under strong small-sample assumptions; not individual prediction or visual-accuracy confidence.
  • Worm Fisher interval is from rounded r and n; genetic relatedness may violate independent-strain assumption.
  • Octopus aggregate choices cannot provide an animal-level confidence interval. Polarization transmission is ideal optics.
  • Barn owl subspecies/taxonomic scope and photopic luminance retained. Painted turtle action-spectrum maximum is not a pigment peak.
  • Shore crab thesis identified separately from peer-reviewed papers. One-channel MSP is not proof of absence of other receptors.
  • No exact animal-view, UV recovery, polarization measurement, chemical sensing or 95% perceptual accuracy is claimed from RGB.
  • Studies without adequate samples retain an unavailable statistical interval. Independent predictive validation remains pending for all ten sandbox models.

The phone supports local image observation and hypothesis exploration. It cannot measure UV, full spectra, polarization or chemical concentration in this implementation. Validation requires appropriately calibrated independent datasets.
