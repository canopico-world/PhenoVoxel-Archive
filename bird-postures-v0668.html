Science and limits

Sato et al. (2007) includes emperor-penguin field stroke data and describes upstroke/downstroke propulsion in penguins. Williams et al. (2012) supplies emperor-penguin stroke and dive-energetics context. We use these as qualitative biological context: neither study certifies these model angles or timing. The swim study illustrates paired dorsoventral strokes of stiff flippers, a forward-facing beak, belly-down posture and trailing feet, followed by a held glide.

New Zealand Birds Online and the Australian Antarctic Program inform adult external appearance. The restored toes, tarsi and flippers are authored reconstructions, not scanned live-animal measurements. No ice, disc or pedestal belongs to the corrected penguin scene.

Cornell Lab’s adult eagle photographs inform the standing interpretation. The model uses a compact folded-wing morph, closed tail, rebalanced legs and a quiet head turn. It switches between standing and flight studies without pretending to simulate landing or the feather-folding transition. Balance forces, grip and ground pressure are not computed.

Playback seconds are display time. Wing and flipper angles, optical response and joint motion remain uncalibrated. Oxygen use, heart rate, heat exchange, muscle metabolism, fluid forces and buoyancy are not simulated. Independent scientific validation remains pending; no accuracy percentage is claimed.

Review and preservation

Views above are actual model renders, not photographs or generated animal images. Review covers feet from the front and side, both flipper stroke directions, held glide, standing wing shape, head motion, deterministic seeking and restoration of the flight exterior. Cached camera bounds include the revised poses.
