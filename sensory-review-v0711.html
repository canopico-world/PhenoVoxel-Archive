Sense & Sensibility · 0.7.11

Different ways of gathering light.

Ten evidence-linked scene experiments explore mirrors, coarse spatial sampling, directional light, pupil area, spectral mechanisms, adaptation and temporal integration.

PhenoVoxel research status: computational hypotheses and explanatory displays. They are not animal-eye photographs, perceptual validation, new biological discoveries or independent peer review. No 95% confidence interval for subjective appearance is supported.

The repetitive flower drawing has been removed. All 28 enabled visual labs start with a habitat photograph; flowers remain appropriate references for bees and monarchs. Habitat structure is illustrative: location, body-scale viewpoint, natural radiance and water conditions are not necessarily matched to the species. Unknown sensory channels remain under maintenance.

Ordinary phone RGB cannot recover ultraviolet, polarization, thermal infrared or sonar. Some studies retain RGB because the evidence does not justify recoloring it; the controls identify precisely what is calculated.

Great scallop

Pecten maximus · Open specimen & sensory lab →

A mirror and two retinal planes

Published evidence: A layered guanine mirror focuses light onto two retinal layers with different field coverage.

Implemented experiment: Select complementary central/peripheral sharpness masks on the same seabed photo. Mask radius and defocus are user assumptions, not a fitted scallop optical model.

Assigned central-field radius: 0.400 image fraction

Assigned defocus: 12.000 analysis pixels

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "center": 0.4,
  "blur": 12,
  "plane": 0
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: No ray-traced eye, measured point-spread function, eye positions or neural fusion. The two panes are explanatory optical hypotheses, not two conscious views.

Validation needed: Fit measured mirror geometry and retinal positions, then compare spatial predictions with independent behavior.

  • Palmer et al. (2017) · The image-forming mirror in the eye of the scallop

Australian box jellyfish

Chironex fleckeri · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Changing the light admitted by an eye

Published evidence: This species constricts its pupils in light and has shown avoidance of dark objects in laboratory observations.

Implemented experiment: Relative aperture area equals the square of the assigned diameter ratio. Multiply linear RGB by this area ratio as an exposure illustration.

Relative admitted light (fixed irradiance): 0.360 fraction

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "diameter": 0.6
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: Camera auto exposure, retinal adaptation and scatter are omitted. No measured constriction time or acuity is fitted; no temporal parameters borrowed from other box jellyfish.

Validation needed: Digitize species-specific pupil-area time series with uncertainty and compare an independently measured irradiance response.

  • Seymour & O’Hara (2020) · Pupillary response in three box jellyfish
  • Hamner et al. (1995) · Swimming and vision in Chironex fleckeri
spatial · computational hypothesis

Crown-of-thorns sea star

Acanthaster planci · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Large reef structures through coarse spatial sampling

Published evidence: Studies published as A. planci report approximately 8° interommatidial spacing and visually guided orientation toward reef structures.

Implemented experiment: Assign a horizontal field angle to the photograph; average linear RGB within angular cells. The 8° starting value anchors a scenario to reported eye sampling, not a calibrated point-spread function.

Horizontal pooling cells: 8.000 cells

Assumed scene span: 60.000 degrees

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "fov": 60,
  "angle": 8
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: The A. planci species complex has changed taxonomically. Population transfer, compound-eye geometry, blue sensitivity and slow temporal processing remain unresolved. Pooling is not a detection threshold.

Validation needed: Use locality-matched animals and measured receptive fields; test independent reef and contrast scenes rather than matching this illustration.

  • Petie et al. (2016) · Visual orientation by crown-of-thorns starfish
  • Petie et al. (2016) · Crown-of-thorns starfish have image-forming vision
spatial · computational hypothesis

Purple sea urchin

Strongylocentrotus purpuratus · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Spatial information without a camera-like eye

Published evidence: Purple urchins oriented toward or away from 10° dark targets; 6.5° targets did not produce significant orientation in that experiment.

Implemented experiment: Linear-RGB angular pooling of a rocky habitat. The 10° default is an illustrative scale suggested by a target-detection experiment, not measured receptor spacing.

Horizontal pooling cells: 6.000 cells

Assumed scene span: 60.000 degrees

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "fov": 60,
  "angle": 10
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: Distributed photosensitivity is not a single retinal image. The photograph covers one direction only; body screening, direction of travel and approach versus avoidance are not predicted.

Validation needed: Model body and spine screening, and test different target contrasts and independent animals. Do not convert target diameter directly into grating acuity.

  • Yerramilli & Johnsen (2010) · Spatial vision in purple sea urchins

Mediterranean planarian

Schmidtea mediterranea · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Direction and intensity, plus a separate UV response

Published evidence: Planarians compare light intensity using cup-shaped eyes; a separate body-mediated UV-A response was demonstrated.

Implemented experiment: Collapse each assigned image sector to its mean sRGB-derived luminance. Sector count is a display assumption, not the number of photoreceptors.

Assigned directional sectors: 4.000 sectors

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "sectors": 4
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: This is an information diagram, not a worm’s image. UV-A is unmeasured, intensity at the animal is unknown, and behavioral choice is not inferred.

Validation needed: Compare calibrated light gradients and ocular/extraocular responses in independent intact animals. No animal intervention is requested.

  • Shettigar et al. (2017) · Ocular and extraocular sensing in a flatworm

European rabbit

Two cone mechanisms in a landscape

Published evidence: Behavioral spectral modelling supports blue and green cones alongside rods; receptor distribution varies across the retina.

Implemented experiment: Camera blue and green are displayed as blue and yellow, omitting camera red. The separate monochromatic calculation uses nominal 425/523 nm A1 alpha-band pigment templates; chromophore mixture and ocular filters are omitted.

Nominal pigment 425 nm: 0.037 relative absorbance

Nominal pigment 523 nm: 0.888 relative absorbance

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "wavelength": 500
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: Camera channels are not rabbit cones. No calibrated color matching, rod interaction, panoramic field or visual-streak reconstruction. Screen hues do not name rabbit sensations.

Validation needed: Use calibrated reflectance spectra, ocular transmission and brightness-controlled rabbit discrimination data.

Bluegill

Lepomis macrochirus · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Green and long-wave channels in freshwater

Published evidence: Measured green and red cone pigments differ from the action-spectrum peaks obtained under chromatic adaptation; visual resolution also changes with fish size.

Implemented experiment: Camera red and green supply a two-channel screen proxy (R, G, G). A separate nominal 536/620 nm pigment calculation uses a hypothetical monochromatic stimulus and an A1 alpha-band template; any A2 contribution is omitted.

Nominal pigment 536 nm: 0.763 relative absorbance

Nominal pigment 620 nm: 0.121 relative absorbance

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "wavelength": 500
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: This is not a calibrated bluegill color transform. Chromophore mixture, ocular filtering, size, water optics and neural adaptation are not fitted; no juvenile UV claim is inferred.

Validation needed: Use age- and size-matched spectra and held-out prey tasks. Keep MSP and behavioral action spectra separate.

  • Hawryshyn et al. (1988) · Color vision in bluegill
  • Li et al. (1985) · Fish size, resolution and prey selectivity
  • Govardovskii et al. (2000) · Visual pigment templates
genetic · computational hypothesis

Japanese rice fish

Oryzias latipes · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Opsins, adaptation and behavior can disagree

Published evidence: LWS-deficient medaka lost part of the long-wave optomotor response when light adapted; dark adaptation changed the result. Seasonal opsin regulation also affects behavior.

Implemented experiment: Keep the habitat photograph as RGB reference. Compare a selected wavelength with the published light-adapted assay endpoints of 740 and 830 nm; no response curve is interpolated.

Relative to mutant assay endpoint: 40.000 nm above 740 (negative = below)

Relative to control assay endpoint: -50.000 nm above 830 (negative = below)

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "wavelength": 780
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: The endpoints are not universal visibility cutoffs, confidence bounds, or thermal sensing. Strain, adaptation and stimulus power matter; phone RGB cannot reveal missing spectral channels.

Validation needed: Use trial-level intensity, adaptation and genotype data and independent assays. Do not treat gene count as perceived color dimensionality.

  • Homma et al. (2017) · Protanopia in medaka
  • Shimmura et al. (2017) · Seasonal plasticity in color perception
gain · computational hypothesis

American lobster

Homarus americanus · Open specimen & sensory lab →

An eye whose sensitivity changes with internal time

Published evidence: Adult lobster electroretinograms recorded in constant darkness showed increased responses during subjective night.

Implemented experiment: Multiply linear RGB by an assigned gain and report clipped-channel fraction. This is a display experiment; gain is not fitted to the published ERG amplitude.

Assigned linear gain: 1.800 × (not an ERG fit)

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "gain": 1.8
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: No new photons or hidden details are recovered. ERG response, detection threshold and perceived brightness are different quantities. No circadian phase is inferred from device time.

Validation needed: Fit individual circadian records and noise, then predict independent behavioral thresholds at matched illumination.

  • Watson, Jenks & Whitworth (2022) · Circadian lobster visual sensitivity
temporal · computational hypothesis

Leatherback sea turtle

Dermochelys coriacea · Open specimen & sensory lab →

Motion and temporal integration

Published evidence: Isolated hatchling retinas did not follow light modulation above 10 Hz in one study. Aquatic eye anatomy does not establish exceptional night vision.

Implemented experiment: Average a constant horizontal translation over an assigned exposure interval. Smear angle = speed × integration time; scene width converts this into pixels.

Assigned motion smear: 2.000 degrees

Horizontal scene fraction: 0.033 fraction

Starting assumptions and reproducibility
{
  "fov": 60,
  "speed": 20,
  "integration": 100
}

Change controls, compare against Original RGB, reset, and export the assumptions. Reproduce numeric defaults with node sensory-analysis-v0711.mjs.

Limits: The integration interval is an assumption, not 1/10 Hz. No adult temporal response, camera-derived velocity, underwater radiance or gain in photon sensitivity is established. The scene stays still; no flashing stimulus is emitted.

Validation needed: Fit an impulse response from raw age-matched ERG data, then compare independent moving-target behavior. Keep hatchling and adult evidence separate.

  • Horch & Salmon (2009) · Hatchling turtle retinal frequency responses
  • Brudenall et al. (2008) · Leatherback ocular morphology

What would improve these predictions?

Acquire calibrated spectra, measured eye geometry and age/locality-matched response data. Fit optical and receptor models separately from behavior. Test predictions on held-out animals or independent published datasets, report sensitivity to assumptions, and estimate confidence intervals only for clearly defined measured or fitted quantities. An appealing image or a successful software test does not validate a percept.

Sources, limits and candidate validation tests are recorded per species. No animal experiments were conducted for this release. Real-device camera behavior and independent biological review remain separate validation tasks.
